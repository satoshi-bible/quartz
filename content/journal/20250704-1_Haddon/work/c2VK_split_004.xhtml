Chapter 5   The Arrow and the Target

Exercise 5.1   Moving from the Exegetical Idea to the Theological and the Homiletical Ideas

1. Genesis 15:1–21

Exegetical idea: Abram is declared righteous (in right relationship) because he trusts God to deliver on his promises.

Theological idea: When believers trust God to deliver on his promises, they’re brought into a right relationship with him.

Homiletical idea: If we’ve become friends of Jesus, let’s trust him with our lives.

2. 2 Samuel 11:1–12:31

Exegetical idea: When David abused his power by compelling Bathsheba to come to him and murdering Uriah, he was judged by God; when David properly used his power to defeat the enemies of God’s people, he was honored.

Theological idea: Believers who abuse their power to manipulate or hurt others face God’s discipline; those who use their power to advance God’s purposes will be rewarded.

Homiletical idea: Use your power to serve God and others, rather than using it to hurt others and serve yourself.

3. Psalm 124

Exegetical idea: When Israel was delivered from oppression by God’s mighty power, they praised the Lord for his marvelous help.

Theological idea: Since almighty God always helps his people when they are in need, they should praise him.

Homiletical idea: Let’s praise almighty God for the help he’s given us this year.

4. Daniel 3:1–30

Exegetical idea: When confronted with the fiery furnace for their refusal to bow to Nebuchadnezzar’s statute, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego showed their faith in God’s power and their full devotion to him, regardless of the consequences.

Theological idea: The fires of life allow believers to show their faith and full devotion to God, regardless of the outcome.

Homiletical idea: Our faith and full devotion to God stand out in the fires of life.

5. Luke 12:13–34

Exegetical idea: Because the rich fool and his unexpected death illustrate the stupidity of greed, Christians should seek God’s kingdom and give generously of their resources to those in need.

Theological idea: Believers can avoid the foolishness of greed by centering themselves on Christ’s kingdom and living lives of generosity.

Homiletical idea: Since it’s silly to store up stuff, let’s center ourselves on Christ’s kingdom and be extravagantly generous.

6. Acts 10:1–48

Exegetical idea: God gave both Cornelius and the apostle Peter specific visions so that Peter could give the good news of the gospel to all the gentiles of Cornelius’s household.

Theological idea: God moves in the lives of both the lost and the saved in order to get the saved to give the good news of the gospel to the lost.

Homiletical idea: God moves in us, through us, and beyond us, so we can bring the gospel to all kinds of people.

7. Ephesians 1:15–23

Exegetical idea: The Ephesian believers will grow into their Christian identity as they deepen their intimacy with God, grasp their eternal destiny with him, and leverage his divine energy.

Theological idea: Believers can enhance their identities in Christ by deepening their intimacy with God, grasping their eternal destiny with him, and leveraging his divine energy.

Homiletical idea: Our identity in Christ will grow stronger as we deepen our intimacy, grasp our destiny, and leverage his energy.

8. James 2:14–26

Exegetical idea: Unlike false believers, who claim faith but show no evidence of good works, true Christians demonstrate saving faith through sacrificial obedience to God and risky love toward others.

Theological idea: False believers may claim faith but are spiritually dead because they have no works; true believers exhibit spiritual life by sacrificing for God and loving other people.

Homiletical idea: A true and saving faith in Jesus always works for God and others.

Exercise 5.3   A Lot Is at Stake!

  1. Matthew 6:1—At stake is missing out on a reward from God.

  2. Deuteronomy 4:1—At stake is no place to live, not even an apartment!

  3. Ephesians 4:11–16—At stake is the church’s frailty caused when these gifts are not deployed. Also at stake is believers not living a life “worthy of the calling you have received” (v. 1). The benefit for the entire church is that the body is healthy when the gifts are deployed.

Exercise 5.4   The Power of Purpose

  1. The evaluated behavior is that Christians don’t share the gospel and don’t know how to share the gospel.

  2. The evaluated behavior is that listeners don’t think they need to study the Bible, nor do they study it.

  3. The evaluated behavior is that listeners don’t know the spiritual gifts or the ones they possess.

(Note: The above behaviors could be evaluated as existing in the listeners and, therefore, as needing reinforcement. If the behaviors do not exist in the audience, the purpose statements may be stated differently.)
